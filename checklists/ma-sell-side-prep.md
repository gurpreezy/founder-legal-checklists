# M&A Sell-Side Prep Checklist

When an acquirer comes knocking, the company that's already organized negotiates from strength. The one scrambling to assemble records negotiates from weakness — and weakness shows up in the price and the terms. Prepare before you need to.

> Educational checklist, not legal advice. Deeper reading at [blegal.ai — Mergers & Acquisitions](https://blegal.ai/knowledge/mergers-acquisitions).

## Clean up before the process
- [ ] Cap table reconciled to signed docs (every dollar of the purchase price flows through it)
- [ ] All IP assignments complete — the #1 acquirer diligence flag
- [ ] Convertibles (SAFEs, notes) mapped to how they're treated on a change of control
- [ ] Customer contracts reviewed for **change-of-control / assignment** clauses that could need consents

## Know your own waterfall
- [ ] Model who gets paid what at the deal price — liquidation preferences, participation, seniority
- [ ] Understand how option holders and unvested equity are treated
- [ ] Identify any preferences that change the founder's actual take

## Watch the deal-specific traps
- [ ] **Earnout terms** — who controls the post-close business that determines whether you hit them?
- [ ] **Escrow / holdback** — how much of the price is withheld, and for how long?
- [ ] **Working-capital adjustment** — the post-closing true-up that quietly moves real money
- [ ] **Reps, warranties, and indemnification** — your exposure after the deal closes
- [ ] **MAC clause** — what lets the buyer walk between signing and closing?

## The founder-side principle
The acquirer's draft is written for the acquirer. Every one of the items above is negotiable, and the leverage to negotiate them comes from being organized and informed before the term sheet — not after.
