# Series A Term Sheet Negotiation Checklist

A term sheet is mostly non-binding, but it sets the anchor for everything in the definitive documents. The terms that matter split into two buckets: **economics** (who gets what money) and **control** (who decides what). Founders often over-focus on valuation and under-focus on control.

> Educational checklist, not legal advice. Deeper reading at [blegal.ai — How to prepare for Series A](https://blegal.ai/knowledge/04-series-a-preparation) and the [term glossary](https://blegal.ai/knowledge/glossary).

## Economics
- [ ] **Valuation & price per share** — pre-money vs post-money; know which the number refers to
- [ ] **Option pool** — size and, crucially, whether it's created in the pre-money (dilutes you) or post-money
- [ ] **Liquidation preference** — aim for 1x non-participating; resist participating preferred and multiples
- [ ] **Anti-dilution** — broad-based weighted average is standard; resist full ratchet
- [ ] **Dividends** — non-cumulative if possible

## Control
- [ ] **Board composition** — count the seats; who controls the board after this round?
- [ ] **Protective provisions** — which decisions need investor approval (financings, sale, charter changes, pool increases)?
- [ ] **Drag-along** — what threshold can force a sale, and does it protect the minority's downside?
- [ ] **Founder vesting** — is your stock being put back on a vesting schedule? On what terms?
- [ ] **Information & pro rata rights** — reasonable, but watch the cumulative burden across investors

## The framing that helps
- [ ] Decide your **two or three non-negotiables** before the call — usually a control item, not the valuation
- [ ] Model the **liquidation waterfall** at a few exit prices to see what the preference actually costs you
- [ ] Remember a higher valuation with a participating 2x preference can be worse than a lower clean deal
