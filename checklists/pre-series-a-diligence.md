# Pre-Series A Legal Diligence Checklist

When a venture fund decides to lead your Series A, their counsel sends a diligence request list. The deals that close fast are the ones where the founder can answer it in days, not weeks. This is what they will ask for — get ahead of it.

> Educational checklist, not legal advice. See the full analysis at [blegal.ai — How Should Founders Prepare for Series A?](https://blegal.ai/knowledge/04-series-a-preparation)

## Corporate records
- [ ] Certificate of incorporation and all amendments
- [ ] Bylaws, as currently in effect
- [ ] Board and stockholder consents/minutes since formation
- [ ] Cap table reconciled to signed documents (not just your cap-table software)
- [ ] Good-standing certificate from your state of incorporation

## Equity and securities
- [ ] All SAFE, convertible note, and warrant agreements
- [ ] Stock purchase agreements and vesting schedules for every founder
- [ ] 83(b) election filings for all founders and early holders
- [ ] Option plan, board approvals for every grant, and 409A valuations
- [ ] Evidence each securities issuance had a valid exemption from registration

## Intellectual property
- [ ] Signed IP assignment from every founder, employee, and contractor
- [ ] Confirmation no IP was developed at a prior employer or university
- [ ] Trademark and domain ownership in the company's name
- [ ] Open-source usage that could affect proprietary code

## Contracts and people
- [ ] Customer and vendor contracts with change-of-control or assignment terms
- [ ] Employment and contractor agreements; confirm at-will status
- [ ] Any debt, loans, or liens against the company

## The two that sink deals
- [ ] **IP assignment gaps** — a contractor who never assigned their work is the single most common Series A problem.
- [ ] **Cap table that doesn't reconcile** — if your records don't match signed docs, every number is suspect.
